# The channel

Supersedes `CHARACTER-BIBLE.md`, which was written before the reference channel had
been watched and aims at the wrong thing. Keep that file for the production
discipline; ignore its tone.

---

## 1. What the reference actually does

Five videos from @santeluca, watched:

- One stylized **3D game-render** character. Not photoreal, not filmed. Same white
  hair, same goggles, same red sweater in every video.
- Surreal-luxury settings: a Maybach interior, a glass pod above the clouds.
- **Deadpan absurdist monologue** — Beyblade launchers, a single quail egg shared
  between four people, "my supplier from Wuhan", "a small private military".
- 23–35 seconds. 4:3, SD resolution, deliberately.
- Big white captions with a black outline. Not premium typography — TikTok default.
- **The last line spoken is the line printed on the shirt.** Then a hard cut to a
  real person wearing it on a white background, and the URL.

The engine is that last beat. Thirty seconds of setup so that one sentence lands,
and the sentence is the product. You cannot hear the joke without seeing the shirt.

## 2. Where ours differs, and why it's better

The reference's lines are funny. Ours are sad:

> They built me for a colder year.
> I am the last one in this colour.
> Made for a place that closed.

A comedy setup that pays off in an absurd line gets a laugh. A comedy setup that
pays off in a *melancholy* line gets a laugh and then takes it back — and that is
the one people rewatch, screenshot, and send to someone.

**So the format is: brag, brag, brag, then the line turns it.**

Thirty seconds of a man describing something ridiculous and specific with total
confidence. Then one sentence that reveals the whole thing is gone. The humour and
the sadness run on the same engine — you do not switch between them, the last line
just re-reads everything above it.

## 3. The character

**The Last Customer.**

He is the only remaining patron of things that have ended. The restaurant closed.
The mill stopped running that colour. The season is over. Nobody has told him, or
he knows and is continuing anyway.

- Male, late thirties, severe, immaculate. Stylized 3D render — hard edges, game
  cinematic, **not** photoreal. Photoreal is where AI faces break and it is also
  the wrong register.
- He speaks in the **present tense about things that no longer exist**. That is the
  whole comedy engine. "My tailor does four fittings." He does not. He is dead.
- Total confidence. No irony, no wink, no awareness that anything is wrong. The
  moment he seems sad on purpose, it dies.
- He is never *poor*. Whatever ended, he is still in the middle of it, still
  spending. The wealth is what makes the ending funny instead of pitiable.
- He addresses one person. Never an audience, never "you guys".

### Settings

Impossible, immaculate, empty interiors — a dining room with one table laid for
twelve, an airport lounge with no aircraft, a lit shop floor at night with no
stock. **Always empty apart from him.** That does the work of the concept without
a word of explanation.

Lock two or three and reuse them. Every new location multiplies the consistency
problem.

## 4. Format spec

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 25–35 seconds |
| Frame | 4:3, deliberately soft, SD-ish. Grain and compression are the look |
| Shots | 3–5 seconds each. AI video is strong at four seconds and falls apart at twenty |
| Captions | Big, white, outlined, centred low. TikTok default — do not make them tasteful |
| Voice | Close, level, unhurried. No music under dialogue |
| Ending | Line spoken → hard cut → real person in the garment on white → URL |
| Disclosure | Label it as AI-generated. TikTok requires it and a stylised channel loses nothing |

**The last frame is a real photograph of a real garment on a real person.** After
thirty seconds of render, the cut to something physical is what makes it a brand
instead of an animation. Do not skip this to save an afternoon.

## 5. Ten scripts

Each ends on the line printed on the piece being sold. `[ ]` are shots.

### 1 — Long Sleeve · *They built me for a colder year*

> **[Empty dining room, one table laid for twelve. He sits at the head.]**
> The heating in this building runs on a schedule from 1974.
> **[Closer. He straightens a cuff.]**
> Every October, whether anyone is here or not, it comes on at six in the morning
> and it does not stop until April.
> **[He looks off-camera at the empty chairs.]**
> I pay for all of it. I have asked them not to change it.
> **[Straight to lens.]**
> They built me for a colder year.

> **[He walks a rail, touching nothing.]**

> **[Beat.]**
> The dye house is flats now.

### 3 — Hoodie · *Made for a place that closed*

> **[An airport lounge. Immaculate. No aircraft through the windows.]**

> **[He sits. Crosses his legs.]**
> Fourteen years. He never once asked my name.
> **[Small satisfaction.]**

> **[Beat. To lens.]**
> Made for a place that closed.

### 4 — Crewneck · *I was somebody's winter*

> She said I was difficult in November and impossible by January.
> **[He adjusts the mirror, looks at himself, not the road.]**

> **[Beat.]**
> She did not stay for spring.
> **[To lens.]**
> I was somebody's winter.

### 5 — Joggers · *This was cut for someone taller*

> **[A fitting room. Three mirrors. He stands in the middle.]**
> My tailor does four fittings. Everyone else does two. He says two is for people

> **[He turns. Considers.]**
> He is ninety-one.
> **[Beat.]**
> He has not answered the telephone since February.
> **[To lens.]**
> This was cut for someone taller.

### 6 — Beanie · *I remember the sun being closer*

> **[He holds up nothing. Turns it over anyway.]**

> **[Beat.]**

> **[To lens.]**

## 6. Writing rules

- **Present tense for dead things.** This is the engine. "My tailor does four
  fittings", not "did".
- **One specific number per script.** Fourteen years. Forty minutes. Ninety-one.
  Specificity is what makes an invented world feel remembered.
- **He never explains the joke and never acknowledges the loss.** The audience does
  that work. If he seems to know it's sad, it stops being funny and stops being sad.
- **The turn is one sentence.** Never two. It arrives, and the video ends.
- **The last line is always the shortest line.**
- Never: "guys", "link in bio", any brand from our world, any date after 2010 said
  aloud, any word that names an emotion.

## 7. Production order

1. Publish the products. Nothing else matters until something can be bought.
2. Photograph a real sample on a real person, white background. This is the last
   frame of every video.
3. Lock the character: three angles, one outfit, one grade. Reuse forever.
4. Lock two locations. Not five.
5. Generate in 3–5 second shots. Assemble in the edit. Never generate long.
6. Sound last, and give it real time. It is half the result.
