# IRREGULAR — Character & Channel Bible

> **Working name.** `IRREGULAR` is a placeholder until domains and the TikTok handle
> are checked. In the garment trade an *irregular* is a factory second — a flawed
> item pulled from the line. In this world, it is also a person. Everything below
> survives a rename; only the wordmark changes.

---

## 1. The world

Clothing is issued, not bought.

Every citizen receives **three garments a year** from the Bureau of Standard Dress.
Same cut, same colour, same size bands. Each garment carries a **collar tag** — a
woven RFID strip that records issue date, ward, and expiry. When a garment expires
it stops being yours. You surrender it. You get the next one.

The tag is the point. Clothing is not clothing here, it is *documentation*.

**Altering an issued garment is a criminal offence.** The charge is *defacement of
state property*. In practice nobody is jailed for a hemmed sleeve — they are fined,
re-banded, and moved down the issue queue. The punishment is administrative, which
is worse. It never ends.

Three rules that must never be broken in a script:

1. **Nobody buys clothes. They receive them.** The word "shop" doesn't exist.
2. **The tag is sacred to the state and worthless to the Tailor.** It is the villain.
3. **The Bureau is never seen.** No soldiers, no drones, no chase scenes. Only
   paperwork, notices, and the sound of someone else's footsteps upstairs.

The dystopia is bureaucratic, not apocalyptic. No ruins. Everything works. That is
what makes it frightening, and it is far cheaper to shoot.

---

## 2. The character

**The Tailor.** No name is ever given. His workshop is **Room 12**, two floors under
a laundry, and people refer to him by the room, not the man. "Take it to Twelve."

- Late fifties. Reading glasses pushed up. Sleeves rolled, always.
- Hands are scarred, precise, and constantly working. **The hands are the star.**
- Speaks the way a surgeon narrates: quiet, exact, unhurried, faintly amused.
- Has done this long enough to be bored of the risk, which is the whole charisma.
- He is not a rebel. He has no politics. He simply thinks badly-made things are an
  insult, and he fixes them. That is more interesting than a revolutionary and it
  keeps the channel from being preachy.

### Why his face is mostly out of frame

He is shot from the chest down, over the shoulder, or in hard partial light — a jaw,
a hand, the corner of a mouth. Full face appears maybe **once every ten videos**, and
it lands hard when it does.

This is a story choice *and* a production choice. Faces are where AI video breaks:
morphing features across shots is the single clearest "this is fake" signal. Hands on
fabric, tools, and cloth hold up far better and read as expensive. The mystery and the
technical constraint point the same direction — take it.

---

## 3. The mark

Every real garment sold carries the same detail:

**A red stitch where the collar tag was cut out.**

Four or five stitches, oxide red, at the inside back neck. That's it. That's the brand.

- Lore: it is the scar left when he removes the Bureau tag. It says *this was theirs.*
- Video: it is the shot that ends most videos. He cuts, he stitches, hard cut, black.
- Production: it is one small embroidery placement. Cheap, repeatable, and it makes a
  plain heavyweight tee into a branded object without a logo anywhere on the outside.
- Commerce: it is why someone pays €55 for a blank tee. They are buying the scar.

No outer logos. Ever. The whole premise is a world where the label on the outside is
the state's. The brand lives on the inside of the neck.

---

## 4. The wardrobe (locked)

Six pieces. He wears these and only these, forever. A locked wardrobe keeps the
character consistent on screen, keeps the AI reference set small, and keeps the
product line small enough to actually sample and stock.

| # | Piece | Description | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Issue Tee** | Heavyweight boxy tee, bone or ash. Tag cut, red stitch. | Hero product, entry price |
| 2 | **Room 12 Overshirt** | Waxed or dry canvas chore coat, charcoal. Patched elbow, deep chest pockets. | Signature silhouette |
| 3 | **The Liner** | Quilted vest, worn under the overshirt. Olive drab. | Layering, autumn drop |
| 4 | **Standard Trouser** | Wide utility trouser, cropped, ash. Reinforced knee. | Completes the fit |
| 5 | **Watch Cap** | Ribbed beanie, bone. Red stitch on the fold. | Cheap, high-margin |
| 6 | **Tool Roll** | Canvas roll / pouch. | Accessory, gift price point |

**Colour discipline:** bone, ash, charcoal, olive drab. One accent only — oxide red,
used exclusively for the stitch. Never a fifth colour. Never a bright anything.

## 5. Look, sound, and cut

**Camera.** One location, three angles, forever: overhead on the workbench, a low
three-quarter across the table, and a tight insert on the hands. Static or a slow
push. No handheld, no whip pans, no drone. Restraint is what reads as premium.

**Light.** One warm practical over the bench, one cold spill from a stairwell.
Everything else falls off to black. Deep shadow hides everything AI does badly.

**Grade.** Crushed blacks, desaturated, warm highlights against cold shadow. Fine
grain over everything, consistent every video. Build it once as a preset, never
deviate — a consistent grade across a grid of videos is most of what "premium" means
on a profile page.

**Sound.** This carries more than the picture:
- Room tone with a low hum, always.
- Fabric. Scissors. A machine starting. Thread pulled. These are the hooks.
- Voice close-mic'd, near-whisper, no music under it.
- Music only in the last three seconds, if at all.

**Length.** 15–25 seconds. Hard cut to black on the last word — no outro, no logo
sting, no "link in bio" card. The comments will ask.

**Shot length.** Build every video from **3–5 second shots**. AI video is strong at
four seconds and falls apart at twenty. The format is designed around that limit
rather than fighting it.

---

## 6. Voice rules

- Short declaratives. He never explains twice.
- No slang, no era-markers, no brand names from our world.
- Never says "buy", "link", "drop", "collab", "guys", "fam".
- Dry humour lands in the *gap*, not the punchline. He states an absurd rule flatly
  and moves on.
- He addresses one person, never an audience. Not "you all" — "you".
- Last line is always the shortest line.

---

## 7. Recurring formats

1. **The Alteration** (the spine, ~60% of output). A garment lands on the bench. What
   it was, what he changed, why that is now a crime. Ends on the red stitch.
2. **Confiscation Notice.** He reads an official notice aloud, deadpan, while working.
   The comedy is entirely in the writing of the notice.
3. **The Drawer.** He opens a drawer of things that shouldn't exist — a pre-Bureau
   button, a zip, a garment with a *brand* on it. Lore delivery.
4. **The Customer.** An off-screen voice sets down a garment and asks for something.
   He answers in one line. Cheapest possible video, highest reply rate.
5. **The Instruction.** How to remove a tag without triggering it. Tense, procedural,
   shot entirely on hands. These are the ones that get saved and shared.

## 8. Twenty hooks

Fully scripted: 1–10. Beat outlines: 11–20.

### 1 — "They gave you three shirts this year."
> **[Hands set a folded bone tee on the bench]**
> They gave you three shirts this year. Same as everyone.
> **[He turns the collar out, finds the tag]**
> This is the part that isn't yours.
> **[Scissors. One cut.]**
> Nine seconds. That's the whole crime.
> **[Needle. Red thread. Four stitches. Cut to black.]**

### 2 — "Defacement of state property."
> **[Overhead. A notice on the bench, weighted with scissors]**
> "Defacement of state property. Fine: forty units."
> **[He slides the notice aside, keeps sewing]**
> They print these on better paper than the shirts.

### 3 — "Somebody's grandmother made this."
> **[He holds a seam to the light]**
> Look at that. Somebody's grandmother made this. Forty years ago. By hand.
> **[Beat]**
> Yours was made by a room with nobody in it.

### 4 — "You wore it wrong on purpose."
> **[A tee on the bench, deliberately shrunk and boxy]**
> You washed it hot. On purpose. To make it fit.
> **[He runs a thumb along the hem]**
> That's not a mistake, that's taste. They can't fine you for that yet.

### 5 — "The tag knows where you sleep."
> **[Tight on the collar tag between two fingers]**
> Issue date. Ward. Expiry.
> **[He turns it over]**
> And a number that isn't any of those.
> **[Sets it down. Doesn't explain.]**

### 6 — "Bring it back when it's yours."
> **[Off-screen voice: "How long?"]**
> **[He doesn't look up]**
> Come back Thursday.
> **[Voice: "And if they check it?"]**
> Then it won't be mine either.

### 7 — "This one expired."
> **[A worn overshirt, folded]**
> This expired in spring. He kept wearing it.
> **[He opens the collar. The tag is already gone — a red stitch there instead]**
> Somebody got to it before me.
> **[Small nod]** Good work, whoever you are.

### 8 — "Four stitches."
> **[Pure procedure. No preamble. Needle, thread, close on hands]**
> In. Out. In. Out.
> **[He bites the thread]**
> Four stitches. Now it's yours.
> **[Black]**

### 9 — "They made it to fall apart."
> **[He pulls at a shoulder seam. It gives immediately]**
> Three washes. That's the design. So you come back in the spring and take another one.
> **[He begins to re-sew it, heavier]**
> This will outlive the Bureau.

### 10 — "You can't return this."
> **[A finished overshirt on a hanger, red stitch visible at the neck]**
> When they ask for it back — and they will ask —
> **[Beat]**
> tell them it isn't theirs any more.

### 11–20 — outlines

11. **The button jar.** He tips out a jar of pre-Bureau buttons. Each one is from a
    garment that no longer exists. He names three of them. Lore, no product.
12. **Sizing.** He explains the state's size bands (there are four) and why everyone
    looks wrong. Ends measuring an actual shoulder properly.
13. **The queue.** Reads out how many months a re-banded citizen waits for issue.
    Absurd number. He keeps working. Never comments.
14. **Wax.** Full procedural: waxing the overshirt canvas over heat. Pure ASMR, near
    zero dialogue. Sound-led. Very high save rate.
15. **The dye bath.** Olive drab going into a pot. "The colour they issue is the
    cheapest one that exists." Product colour reveal disguised as lore.
16. **A child's garment.** Someone leaves a small tee. He removes the tag without a
    word. First moment of visible feeling. Save this one for ~video 25.
17. **Counterfeit.** Someone brings him a fake red stitch — six stitches, wrong red.
    He is offended on craft grounds, not commercial ones.
18. **Inspection.** Footsteps upstairs. He covers the bench with a cloth, sits still,
    the light changes, footsteps recede. He uncovers. Continues. No dialogue at all.
19. **What you're allowed to keep.** He lists the three things the Bureau lets you own
    outright. The third is genuinely unsettling.
20. **The face.** The payoff video. He looks up, straight into the lens, for the first
    time. One line: *"You've been watching a long time. Do you want one or not?"*
    This is the video that opens the store.

## 9. Production pipeline

The order matters. Getting it backwards is how a channel sells nothing.

1. **Design the garment** — real blank, real colourway, real placement of the stitch.
2. **Order one physical sample.** One. Photograph it on a table, flat, in the grade.
3. **Build the reference set** from those photographs — this is what the character
   wears in every video. The clothes on screen must be the clothes in the box.
4. **Lock the character reference** — three angles, the wardrobe, the workshop.
5. **Generate in 3–5 second shots**, assemble in the edit, never generate long.
6. **Sound last, and give it real time.** It is doing half the work.

### Weekly loop once running

Scripts and shot lists get drafted in batches of ten. You approve or kill each one in
a single pass. Generation and assembly run off the approved list. Your recurring input
is: **approve designs, approve videos.** Nothing per order — fulfilment is automatic.

### Two things to sort before scaling

- **AI disclosure.** TikTok requires synthetic media to be labelled. Label it. A
  channel this stylised loses nothing, and an unlabelled one is a takedown risk.
- **Consistency debt.** Every new location, garment, or angle you add multiplies the
  consistency problem. Adding a second room should feel like a budget decision.

---

## 10. Never

- No outer logos.
- No fifth colour.
- No music under dialogue.
- No "link in bio" on screen.
- No second character on camera. Voices only.
- No showing the Bureau.
- No garment on screen that you cannot ship.
