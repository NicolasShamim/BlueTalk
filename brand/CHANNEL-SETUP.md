# Setting up the channels

Checked 9 September 2026. Platforms are the only real authority — confirm at signup.

## 1. The name

| | |
|---|---|
| **soldanyway.com** | ✅ available |
| **@soldanyway** on TikTok | ✅ free |
| **@soldanyway** on YouTube | ✅ free |

Clean across all three, which is rare and is most of the argument.

**Take `soldanyway.com` and `@soldanyway` on both platforms, the same day.**

The handle *is* the domain. Every time it appears — under every video, in every
comment, in every share — it is also the address, so nobody needs to go hunting for
a link in bio.

The name is also the second half of the brand's own sentence: the garment tag says
*failed inspection*, the label says *sold anyway*. It carries the whole idea without
a line of about-us copy, and it is a stance rather than a description, which is what
people actually wear.

## 2. Claiming the accounts

### TikTok

Sign up, set the handle to `soldanyway.com`, then **switch to a Business account**
(Settings → Account → Switch to Business Account).

The reason: personal accounts historically need around 1,000 followers before a
clickable website link is allowed in the bio. Business accounts get the link field
immediately. The trade-off is that Business accounts can only use TikTok's
commercial music library — which costs this channel nothing, because the videos
carry their own sound design and never sit on a trending audio.

- **Name:** SOLD ANYWAY
- **Bio:** the line, then the address. Nothing else:
  > Garments that failed inspection.
  > soldanyway.com
- **Link:** https://soldanyway.com

### YouTube

Create a channel, set the handle to `@soldanyway`.

- **Description:** same two lines, plus one sentence of what the channel is
- **Links:** the site, shown on the channel banner
- **Banner:** `channel/yt-banner.png` — 2560×1440, with everything important inside
  the 1546×423 safe area so it survives every crop

### Both

- **Avatar:** `channel/avatar.png` — tested legible down to 48px
- Same handle, same avatar, same name on both. A person who sees one should find the
  other by typing the same thing.

## 3. Disclosure — not optional

Every video is synthetic and both platforms require that to be declared.

- **TikTok:** turn on the AI-generated content toggle when posting.
- **YouTube:** answer yes to "altered or synthetic content" in the upload flow.

A stylised channel loses nothing by being labelled — nobody thinks the man in the
empty dining room is real. An unlabelled one risks takedown, and takedowns land on
the account, not the video.

## 4. Posting

**Cadence: four or five a week, indefinitely.** Not seven. A channel that posts five
times a week for six months beats one that posts daily for three weeks and stops,
and the second is what actually happens to people.

The format is built for this: one character, two locations, one outfit, 3–5 second
shots. Once the character reference is locked, a video is an evening, not a weekend.

**Same video both places, with one rule: never upload the TikTok-watermarked file to
YouTube.** Export clean from the editor both times. Shorts demotes visibly
watermarked reuploads.

**Burn the captions in** rather than using either platform's auto-captions. The
captions are part of the look — big, white, outlined, centred low — and auto-captions
will not match, will mis-hear the deliberately odd lines, and differ between the two
platforms.

### Captions and hashtags

The on-screen text carries the video. The written caption is small:

- One line, in the character's voice, that is **not** the line in the video. It gives
  people something to reply to.
- Two or three tags at most. `#soldanyway` plus whatever the video is actually about.
  Not thirty. Not #fyp.
- Never put the shop link in the caption of a video. The handle is the address; the
  bio is where the link lives. A caption that sells makes a video look like an ad,
  and this whole format works by not looking like one.

### The first ten

Post the first ten before judging anything. The account has no signal to go on until
then, and the format needs the reps as much as the algorithm does.

## 5. Order

1. Register `soldanyway.com`
2. Claim `@soldanyway` on TikTok and YouTube — same day, before something changes
3. Set avatar, banner, bio, link
4. Point the domain at the site with a `CNAME`
5. Then make videos

Steps 1–3 are twenty minutes and they are the ones with a deadline on them. The rest
has no clock.
