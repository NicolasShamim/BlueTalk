# Money and legal

**Not legal or tax advice.** Orientation, with sources, so the conversation with an
accountant is short. Figures were current when checked and move — confirm before
trading.

---

## 1. How the money actually flows

Fourthwall is **merchant of record**. That means the customer buys from Fourthwall,
not from you.

```
customer pays Fourthwall
   → Fourthwall deducts the product's flat cost + payment processing
   → the remainder is your profit
   → paid out monthly to your bank
```

**No card needs to be linked to sell.** Production cost comes out of the sale price,
not out of your pocket, so nothing is charged to you when an order comes in. This is
the whole appeal of the model — you cannot lose money on an order.

What you do need is a **payout method**. Fourthwall pays through Stripe to a bank
account by transfer or debit card. Payouts run monthly, sent by around the third
business day of the following month, on balances over about $25.

You only need a card for things you buy: **ordering samples**. Which you should do
before selling anything, so budget for that.

### The fees

- Each catalog product has a **published flat cost**, deducted from your sale price.
  No percentage on top — the margin above that cost is yours.
- Plus payment processing, around **2.9% + $0.30** on card transactions; different
  for PayPal and other methods.

So on a €55 tee against a roughly €15 blank, most of the difference is margin, minus
processing. Set prices against the published cost of each specific product rather
than a rule of thumb.

## 2. What Fourthwall covers, and what it does not

**They handle**, as merchant of record:

- Collecting and remitting **sales tax and VAT** across countries — the single
  biggest reason to use them from Belgium
- Checkout, card data, PCI compliance
- The consumer-facing sale, its terms, refunds and disputes
- Production, shipping, delivery problems

**You still handle:**

- **Your own registration and income tax** in Belgium (below)
- **Personal data collected on your own site** — the waitlist form. That is yours,
  not theirs, the moment it starts storing addresses
- Anything you claim in your videos or on your site

## 3. Belgium

Selling commercially in Belgium means registering. You cannot legally trade without
being in the **Kruispuntbank van Ondernemingen (KBO/BCE)**.

If you have another main status — employed, student, pensioner — the route is
**zelfstandige in bijberoep** (self-employed in a complementary capacity), which
exists precisely for this.

Indicative costs, to be confirmed:

| | |
|---|---|
| KBO registration via a business counter (ondernemingsloket) | ~€111.50 |
| Activating the VAT number | ~€70 |
| Social contributions in bijberoep | ~€98.51 per quarter, **only above ~€1,922.16 of annual earnings** |

That last line is the important one: below that threshold the contributions do not
apply, so starting small is not expensive.

### Worth asking an accountant specifically

- Whether early, irregular sales fall under **diverse inkomsten** (miscellaneous
  income, taxed separately) rather than professional income — the answer depends on
  how regular and organised the activity is, and a shop with a posting schedule
  looks regular.
- Whether the **VAT exemption for small businesses** applies at your turnover, and
  what it changes given Fourthwall already remits VAT on the sale itself.
- How Fourthwall's payouts should be booked, given they are the seller and you are
  receiving a profit share rather than sales revenue.

An **ondernemingsloket** (Acerta, Securex, Xerius, Liantis) does the registration
and will answer most of this as part of it.

## 4. The site's own obligations

Independent of Fourthwall, because they are about your site, not their checkout:

- **A privacy notice** — required as soon as the waitlist form stores an email
  address. GDPR applies to one address as much as ten thousand. Drafted at
  `privacy.html`; it needs your real details filled in.
- **Trader identity** — an EU commercial site should say who is behind it: name,
  address, contact, company number once you have one. Currently the site says only
  "Antwerp".
- **Consent and purpose** — the form already states plainly what the list is for and
  promises one email. Keep that promise; it is also the legal basis.

## 5. Sequence

1. Order samples. You need a card for this and nothing else.
2. Register with an ondernemingsloket before taking a single order. ~€180 and a
   short appointment.
3. Add the payout method in Fourthwall.
4. Fill in the privacy page and the trader details.
5. Then publish the products.

Registration is the step people skip and it is the one that is genuinely required.
It is also cheap and quick — it is not a reason to delay, it is a morning.
