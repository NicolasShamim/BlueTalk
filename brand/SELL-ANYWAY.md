# The format, take two

Supersedes the character and script sections of `CHANNEL.md` (§2, §3, §5, §6) —
the melancholy "Last Customer" engine tested weak with a real audience. Keep
`CHANNEL.md` for everything logistical that still holds: the format spec (§4 —
4:3, 25-35s, captions, disclosure), the production order (§7), and the reference
analysis (§1). This file replaces what he says and why.

---

## 1. Why the old engine didn't work

"Brag, brag, brag, then a sad line" reads as a mood, not a person. Nothing to
agree or disagree with, nobody to be a fan of. A melancholy character gets
watched once. He doesn't get a following, and nobody puts his face on a shirt
to say something about themselves by wearing it.

## 2. The character now

**Kaspar knows a guy.** Always a specific one — a narrow, old-world specialist,
a different one each video, introduced by name and trade. Kaspar's actual belief,
stated plainly at least once a video: **nobody should be good at more than one
thing.** The moment something or someone claims to do everything — an app, an
algorithm, a big-box supplier — he's already out. That belief is the whole spine.
It's a little provocative on purpose: plenty of people will find it precious or
impractical, plenty will feel it names something real they'd stopped noticing.
Either reaction is the engine working.

Only Kaspar is ever on screen. The specialists are described, never shown —
which is also why this format needs nothing beyond the two locked plates
(`character/neutral.png`, `character/grin.png`) and props. No new character
work, ever.

## 3. The shape of every video

1. **Introduce the guy.** A name, a trade, one specific detail that proves real
   mastery (a number: forty years, one movement, one brand).
2. **The squeeze.** Two pressures, always both: cheap competition (a fact, not
   an accusation — "does it for a tenth of the price, overnight"), and
   regulation/bureaucracy that fails his work on a technicality despite it
   being genuinely right. This is the pivot — never just price.
3. **The line.** *"I told him: sell it anyway."* Then the belief, stated once,
   plainly — something like *"A man's whole life's work doesn't stop being
   correct just because a form disagrees with it."*
4. **The CTA.** *"You want a guy for your [item]? [Name]'s still working. Hop
   over to soldanyway.com."*

This is also the origin story for the brand name, in-universe: every piece in
the shop is a specific specialist's rejected-on-paper, right-in-practice work.
"Failed inspection, sold anyway" stops being a slogan and becomes a literal
description of what's for sale.

## 4. Rules

- **One guy, one trade, one video.** Never combine two specialists in one script.
- **Competition is a fact, regulation is the villain.** Cheap overseas
  manufacturing gets named plainly and without judgment — that's just the market.
  The thing that actually ends the guy is bureaucracy that can't see past a
  checkbox. Never imply anything about who makes the cheap version or how —
  that's a real, unproven claim about real people, not an opinion, and it's the
  one line this format never crosses, regardless of how the scene is dressed.
- **The belief gets said once, plainly, not softened.** It's what gets quoted.
  Don't hedge it into politeness.
- **Names are specific and a little antique.** Wilfred, not "a friend." The
  specificity is what makes it feel real instead of like a lecture.
- **He's loyal, not needy.** Kaspar isn't asking you to feel sorry for the guy —
  he's telling you what he did about it and daring you to disagree.
