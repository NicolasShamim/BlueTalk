# The line

The reference brand's line is *"You met me at a very Chinese time in my life."*

It works because of four things, and any replacement has to do all four:

1. **It's a swap on a line people already know.** The closing line of *Fight Club* is
   "You met me at a very strange time in my life." Changing one word keeps the
   emotional weight of the original and detonates it. Half the audience feels the
   echo without being able to place it.
2. **It's a confession, not a slogan.** It sounds like something admitted quietly,
   late. Brands shout; this whispers. That's why it reads as cool instead of merch.
3. **It's sayable.** It's the last line of the video. Thirty seconds of absurd
   bragging, then the character says the thing printed on his chest and it cuts to
   the product. You cannot do that with a logo.
4. **It's long.** A sentence across a chest reads as literature. A word reads as a
   band tee.

## The recommendation

> ### I failed inspection and shipped anyway.

This is the one. It is the brand's whole thesis in six words, and it earns the name:
an *irregular* is a garment that failed quality control and got sold regardless.

- **As a confession** it's about a person, not a shirt. Everyone has failed an
  inspection of some kind and carried on. That's the whole appeal.
- **As a punchline** it's perfect for the format. Thirty seconds of a character
  bragging about something insane, then flat to camera: *"I failed inspection and
  shipped anyway."* Cut to product.
- **As a garment** it sets in two lines across a chest and looks like a statement
  rather than a joke, which is what makes people actually wear it.
- **As a brand** it explains IRREGULAR without a single sentence of about-us copy.

Set across two lines:

```
        I failed inspection
        and shipped anyway
```

## The alternates

Kept in the same register — a confession with a manufacturing defect underneath.

| Line | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **They stopped making people like this.** | Pure swagger. Lands hardest as a punchline after an absurd brag. Implies people are manufactured — dystopian without saying so. | Reads closer to a straight flex; less vulnerable, so less interesting on a second viewing. |
| **They built me for a colder year.** | The most beautiful of the set. Melancholy, mysterious, genuinely poetic. | The least funny. Better on the jacket than the tee. |
| **I am the last one in this colour.** | Native to clothing — a colourway and a person at once. Quietly devastating. | Only works while the range is small; breaks if you ever restock. |
| **Manufactured during a shortage.** | Driest of the four. Deadpan, no first person, reads like a stamp on a crate. | Colder. Harder to say aloud as a punchline. |

## The rule going forward

Each piece carries its own line, and each line is the last thing said in the video
that sells it. The line comes first, the garment second. If a line isn't worth
saying out loud at the end of thirty seconds, it isn't worth printing.
