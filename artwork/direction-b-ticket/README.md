# Direction B — the inspection ticket

Two samples only. A direction to choose or reject, not a finished set.

The first artwork (`artwork/*.png`) was a line of italic type and nothing else.
Against the reference product — an ornate dragon emblem with script woven through
it — that reads closer to a plain garment than to something anyone collects.

This direction makes the graphic **a document rather than a slogan**: a factory
quality-control ticket. Header and record number, an inspection heading, the line
as the centrepiece, then the form fields — batch, lot, inspected, **result: FAIL** —
and a stamp reading SOLD ANYWAY.

It earns the name. An *irregular* is a garment that failed inspection and was sold
regardless, so the graphic is the literal paperwork of that. It also fills a chest
the way the reference does, and every element is type or rule — no illustration to
license, redraw, or keep consistent across six pieces.

The batch and lot numbers change per piece, so each garment is a different record.

## What this direction cannot do

It cannot produce an **illustrated emblem** — the dragon equivalent. That needs
either an illustrator or image generation, and it is the one thing that would match
the reference's richness most directly. Everything here is constructed from
typography and rules, which is a real aesthetic, not a substitute for drawing.

## Files

11 × 12in at 300dpi, transparent, bone ink for dark garments. Ink-on-light versions
and the remaining four pieces get rendered once a direction is chosen.
